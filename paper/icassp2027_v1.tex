\begin{abstract}
We address conformal uncertainty quantification --- valid under
exchangeability --- for non-cooperative emitter geolocation, where
calibration needs labels the deployment cannot provide. In simulation, an evidential--conformal pipeline calibrated once and frozen
loses coverage from $0.90$ to $0.19$ under a 3GPP channel-family change,
and under strong multipath its median error grows ninefold while the head
halves its uncertainty. The failure has a label-free signature: the head
departs from the rule linking curve statistics to its reported uncertainty.
On held-out shifts and seeds, three signals built from it detect each
failing family; used as normalizers preassigned by family, they lift
bandwidth loss from $0.49$ to $0.90$ at $2\%$ of the search area, the
channel family only to $0.74$ in this study.
\end{abstract}

\begin{keywords}
Emitter geolocation, TDOA, conformal prediction, uncertainty
quantification, distribution shift
\end{keywords}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Radio-frequency emitter geolocation is observer-centric: rather than a
device computing its own position, a receiver network estimates \emph{where
an emitter is} from its emissions alone. Applications span regulatory
enforcement, interference hunting, search-and-rescue and adversarial
settings, where the emitter is non-cooperative --- unknown, faulty, or
malicious~\cite{npj2026}. The need is operational: global navigation satellite system
(GNSS) interference in the Baltic
region is now localized in the field~\cite{gattis2026}, and
aviation and shipping encounter it
routinely~\cite{clements2023}.

What separates this setting from acoustic source localization and from
cooperative RF positioning is not the unknown waveform but the absent
label. Uncertainty is not yet a standard axis of comparison there~\cite{grumiaux2022,pan2026,piyu2025}, and probabilistic outputs go uncalibrated~\cite{schymura2021}. Recent work giving
localization estimates finite-sample validity operates where labels are
scarce yet obtainable: semi-supervised formulations still use a small
labelled set~\cite{rozenfeld2025,michael2026}, and field guidance advises
\emph{playback from known positions} for
calibration~\cite{kampe2026}. A non-cooperative emitter cannot be asked to play
back from a known position. We therefore draw the calibration set from a synthetic generator, and the exchangeability that split
conformal prediction relies on is broken by construction.

We therefore treat distribution shift as the primary evaluation regime
rather than a robustness afterthought: the conformal constant is calibrated
once and \emph{frozen}, and both the predicted region and the learned
uncertainty are measured as the deployment conditions move away from the
calibration distribution. Coverage stays near nominal across the recalibrated
configurations and collapses under several shifts when calibration is
frozen (Sec.~\ref{sec:guarantee}). The consequential
result is what the model \emph{says} while that happens: on the shifts that
break coverage it under-reports its uncertainty (less than a third of what
is called for), and under strong multipath lowers it outright while the
error grows ninefold.

We find that the failure leaves a measurable, label-free signature.
In the calibration distribution the head follows a stable rule: its
reported uncertainty is a function of three quality statistics of the
generalized cross-correlation with phase transform (GCC-PHAT) curve it is given (cross-validated $R^2=0.54$--$0.60$). We fit
that rule in-distribution and transfer it. Under shift the head
\emph{violates its own rule}, reporting $1.4\x$ to $12\x$ less uncertainty
than the rule implies, while staying faithful on a shift independently
known to be benign ($0.96\x$) and over-alarming, correctly, at low
signal-to-noise ratio (SNR)
($1.7\x$). The violation is computed from the correlation curve and the
head's own output --- no ground truth --- yet it tracks the coverage that
does require ground truth (Spearman $0.74$ over eleven conditions).

In this paper, we (i)~introduce a \emph{rule-transfer test} that measures,
without labels, whether a deployed evidential head still obeys the
input-to-uncertainty mapping it learned in distribution; (ii)~\emph{measure} that
violation across propagation, bandwidth and hardware shifts, with matched
positive and negative controls; and (iii)~build a \emph{graded response}
from three label-free signals, each of which flags one failure family as a
conformal abstention rule and repairs that same family as a conformal
normalizer, so detection and correction share a signal instead of
requiring separate machinery. How much repair is available is set by how
far the shifted scores exceed the calibration support, and, under that
fixed assignment, median corrected regions separate the families we repair
from those we abstain on. Every operating point is frozen before evaluation and
confirmed on held-out generation seeds. We do not claim localization
accuracy: on this synthetic data --- whose forward model classical estimators
invert exactly~\cite{chan1994} --- grid-search SRP-PHAT~\cite{dibiase2001} is more accurate
(median 7.9\,m vs.\ 150.3\,m). Yet the classical route does not escape this
problem: a 20\,m receiver-survey error collapses its coverage
from 0.887 to 0.523 while its confidence statistic stays blind
(area under the ROC curve, AUROC, 0.50), and no learned rule is left to diagnose.

\section{Problem Formulation and Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Problem statement}
\label{sec:problem}
Let $X\in\mathbb{R}^{6\x257}$ be the six GCC-PHAT~\cite{knapp1976} curves
of one snapshot and $p\in\mathbb{R}^2$ the emitter position. A model
outputs $\mu(X)$ and covariances $\Sigma_a(X)$, $\Sigma_e(X)$, which split
conformal turns into a region $C_\alpha(X)$ satisfying
$\mathbb{P}(p\in C_\alpha)\ge 1-\alpha$ whenever calibration and test data
are exchangeable~\cite{angelopoulos2021}. Labelled pairs $(X,p)$ exist only
under the synthetic calibration distribution $P_0$; the deployment
distribution $P_1$ differs and yields no label, since a non-cooperative
emitter discloses no position. Anything computable at deployment is
therefore a function of $X$ and of the model's own output.

\subsection{Data generation and fixed preprocessing}
\label{sec:gen}
No public corpus carries raw multi-receiver in-phase/quadrature (IQ)
recordings with emitter-position
labels.\footnote{Raw IQ is stored at GB--TB scale, sub-nanosecond
synchronization is uncommon, and a position label
presupposes a cooperative transmitter.} We therefore synthesize the
observation chain physically rather than attaching time differences as
labels. Four non-cooperative low-probability-of-intercept/-detection
(LPI/LPD) waveforms are generated at complex
baseband; TDOAs are imprinted as fractional-sample delays via a
frequency-domain phase ramp, after which a tapped-delay-line (TDL-D) channel~\cite{tr38901}
and additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN) are applied (Fig.~\ref{fig:pipe}). Four receivers on an 8\,km-radius
circle observe an emitter drawn uniformly within it ($f_s=2$\,MHz, carrier
1.5\,GHz, SNR $\sim U[-10,20]$\,dB). Free-space path loss (ITU-R P.525) is
recorded as metadata rather than applied.\footnote{The SNR label already
fixes the noise scale and the CRLB reference; applying both would
invalidate the reference.} One SNR label is shared by all receivers;
inter-receiver imbalance is not modelled.

Time-difference information resides in the \emph{relative alignment} of two
receiver signals, which requires a bilinear comparison across lags. Learned
GCC variants (NGCC-PHAT~\cite{berg2022}, FS-GCC~\cite{cobos2020,comanducci2020}) keep that
correlation outside the network for this reason. We keep the whole chain
fixed, which lets us use the time-delay CRLB~\cite{stein1981} as an
analytical reference for $\Sigma_a$. The whitening exponent is not assumed: over
five seeds, full whitening ($\beta=1$) beats $\beta\in\{0,0.5\}$ in
low-SNR conditional coverage ($\Delta\approx0.07$, $p<0.03$).

\subsection{Evidential head and conformal calibration}
\label{sec:uq}
The head belongs to the evidential family~\cite{amini2020}, whose epistemic component
is known to be ordinal rather than calibrated~\cite{juergens2024}.
A 128-dimensional trunk feature $\phi$ enters a single linear layer
emitting a mean $\mu\in\mathbb{R}^2$, the Cholesky factor $L$ of a $2\x2$
scale matrix, and a degrees-of-freedom parameter
$\nu = 8+5\tanh(\cdot)\in(3,13)$; the aleatoric and epistemic covariances
are $\Sigma_a=\nu LL^{\!\top}\!/(\nu-3)$ and
$\Sigma_e=LL^{\!\top}\!/(\nu-3)$. The Normal--Wishart parameterization for
regression was derived for general output dimension by
Malinin~\etal~\cite{malinin2020}, but evaluated only for scalar targets ---
synthetic 1-D data, UCI regression, per-pixel depth. Our target is a
two-dimensional position, and the off-diagonal term --- the tilt of the
error ellipse --- carries the receiver geometry. We tie
the mean-precision parameter to $\nu$ at unit ratio~\cite{meinert2021}, which
removes the shape-parameter non-identifiability and lets us train by negative log-likelihood alone,
without the evidence regularizer~\cite{malinin2020}. Conformal evidential calibration also has scalar
precedent~\cite{duan2025}; ours
uses the Mahalanobis score
\begin{equation}
M(X,y)=\big((y-\mu)^{\!\top}(\Sigma_a{+}\Sigma_e)^{-1}(y-\mu)\big)^{1/2},
\label{eq:maha}
\end{equation}
a scalar, so split conformal prediction (CP) applies: with $Q$ the empirical quantile
of the $n$ calibration scores at level
$\lceil(n{+}1)(1{-}\alpha)\rceil/n$ (NumPy's `higher'
interpolation), the region is
\begin{equation}
C_\alpha(X)=\{\,y : M(X,y)\le Q\,\},
\label{eq:region}
\end{equation}
and it is this constant that Sec.~\ref{sec:guarantee} freezes. A
group-conditional (Mondrian) variant computes one quantile per SNR bin.

\subsection{Distribution shifts and controls}
\label{sec:axes}
Deployment distributions $P_1$ come from the same generator with one factor
changed at a time in graded levels (600 snapshots each), spanning
propagation, emitter bandwidth and receiver hardware
(Fig.~\ref{fig:pipe}). Sampling-clock offset (SCO) up to 100\,ppm is a \emph{negative control}: it
leaves the median error unchanged ($\x1.01$), so a detector that fires on
it is an alarm generator. Low SNR is a \emph{positive control}: it degrades
the estimate through a mechanism the head does represent. Selection and
confirmation use disjoint levels (Sec.~\ref{sec:detect}).

\section{The Guarantee and Its Precondition}
\label{sec:guarantee}

The head's raw regions carry no guarantee and are far too large. Split
conformal calibration supplies one: when calibration and deployment
data are exchangeable, the region \eqref{eq:region} covers with
probability at least $1-\alpha$, finite-sample and
distribution-free. On this head, a single quantile returns coverage to
nominal and removes $84\%$ of the area (Table~\ref{tab:guarantee}).

Marginal validity, however, is silent about conditions: the elliptical
region covers only $0.657$ of the lowest-SNR bin. One quantile per SNR bin (Mondrian
inductive conformal prediction~\cite{vovk2012})
restores that bin to nominal and makes the typical region smaller at the
same time, because the group-conditional constant enlarges the low-SNR
regions and shrinks every other bin (Table~\ref{tab:guarantee}) --- using a
label the deployment does not provide (Sec.~\ref{sec:detect}). These
properties survive the generator: coverage stays within $0.894$--$0.909$
across 13 recalibrated configurations spanning channel family,
line-of-sight/non-line-of-sight (LOS/NLOS) mixture, delay bias and whitening $\beta$.

All of this assumes the calibration set is drawn from the test
distribution. It cannot be (Sec.~\ref{sec:problem}). With the constant
calibrated on $P_0$ and frozen, a channel-family change alone
takes coverage from $0.90$ to $0.19$ (TDL-A) and $0.20$ (TDL-C; five
training seeds, spread $0.15$--$0.24$). The guarantee has not failed; its
precondition has. This paper asks whether the model can tell ---
and, where it can, what can be done about it.

\section{Label-Free Failure Diagnosis}
\label{sec:diag}

Label-free self-diagnosis is itself classical in navigation: receiver
autonomous integrity monitoring (RAIM) tests satellite measurements for mutual consistency~\cite{brown1992},
signal-quality monitoring reads distortions of the correlator
peak~\cite{phelts2001}, and time-difference-of-arrival (TDOA) residual tests flag faulty
sensors~\cite{giunta2023}. Each diagnoses the measurements under a trusted
error model. In machine learning the same question is
drift detection, with labels~\cite{bayram2022} and without~\cite{oikarinen2021}. Here the diagnosed object is the model: whether the head
still obeys its own input-to-uncertainty rule.

\subsection{Fitting the rule}
\label{sec:fit}
On held-out in-distribution data alone --- never used to train the model,
and disjoint from the conformal calibration split --- we regress the head's
reported epistemic scale
$\sigma_{\mathrm{epi}}=(\operatorname{tr}\Sigma_e)^{1/2}$ on three statistics --- the RMS of the windowed
curve, the peak-to-far-sidelobe ratio, the peak half-width --- all
functions of $X$ only:
\begin{align}
\log\sigma_{\mathrm{epi}} = 23.26 &+ 3.02\log \mathrm{featRMS} \nonumber\\[-3pt]
 &- 1.89\log \mathrm{(pk/far)} - 0.99\log \mathrm{hw}.
\label{eq:rule}
\end{align}
Two-fold cross-validated $R^2$ is $0.54$--$0.60$ against $0.57$ in-sample,
with seed-stable coefficients. The fit is an empirical in-distribution summary of the head's behaviour,
not a competing model. Note the
sign on half-width. Under $P_0$ a degraded snapshot has a \emph{narrower}
peak --- bandwidth, not alignment quality, sets the width of a noise
maximum --- so the head learned that narrow means uncertain. That is a fact
about $P_0$, not about physics.

\subsection{Transferring the rule}
\label{sec:transfer}
We apply the fitted rule unchanged to each shifted condition and compare
its prediction with what the head reports (Fig.~\ref{fig:transfer}).
Normalizing so the calibration distribution reads $1.00$: on the negative
control the head still obeys its rule ($0.96$); at low SNR it over-reports
relative to the rule ($1.66$), the correct direction. On every other shift
it under-reports --- TDL-A/-C $0.33/0.28$, fading $0.34$, bandwidth
reduction $0.71$, strong multipath $0.08$ --- while the median error grows
by $1.7\x$ to $15\x$.

The violation is computed from $X$ and the head's own output, with no
labels. It nevertheless ranks the conditions the way the label-dependent
coverage does: Spearman $0.74$ over eleven conditions, $0.85$ with the
positive control removed.

The decomposition separates two failures. Under multipath the rule
sees the degradation and the head ignores it. Under bandwidth reduction the
head follows the rule but the rule is itself blind, because that
shift widens the peak in a direction $P_0$ never contained.

\subsection{Where the violation comes from}
\label{sec:mech}
The violation is located in the feature space: the final feature is a rectified linear unit (ReLU)
output, so its entries are non-negative, and its projection onto the ellipse-size
weights is negative in all five training seeds ($-1.10$ to $-1.15$);
more activation therefore shrinks the predicted covariance exponentially.
Multipath and bandwidth reduction raise activation by $1.10\x$ to $1.32\x$
without improving the signal --- spurious peaks switch on additional
filters --- and the model becomes more confident. The shifts the head does
handle run the same path in reverse: fading and a change of channel family
lower the feature norm ($0.75$--$0.91\x$, five seeds) and the covariance
grows. The sign of that change separates every inversion from every
correctly handled channel shift, and is why the norm returns as a flag in
Sec.~\ref{sec:detect}. (The mechanism is specific to this head; the
rule-transfer test does not depend on it.)

\section{Results: Detection, Repair, and Abstention}
\label{sec:detect}

\subsection{The two limits}
Can the failure be repaired rather than detected? Under deployment
constraints --- one forward pass, a frozen head, calibration from $P_0$ ---
the answer is graded by two limits. The first is the calibration
support: the fraction $\beta^{*}$ of shifted scores beyond the calibration
maximum. Reweighting a fixed score cannot move a finite quantile past that
support~\cite{tibshirani2019} --- at the strongest multipath level
$\beta^{*}$ is $28$--$47\%$ (five seeds) against
$\alpha=0.10$, versus at most $5\%$ on the shifts recalibration does
reach. The same group-conditional machinery that lifts an SNR shift from
$0.157$ to $0.661$ lifts this one only from $0.142$ to $0.199$: the limit
is specific, not generic. We therefore evaluate family-wise normalization and the size of the
corrected region under a gate of one fifth of the search area.

\subsection{Label-free detection}
We turn the violation into an abstention rule. The score
$s=\beta^{\!\top}x-\log\sigma_{\mathrm{epi}}$, the signed violation of
\eqref{eq:rule}, is large when the head claims more certainty than its own
rule allows. Held-out in-distribution data are
split in three --- rule fit, conformal calibration of a
$p$-value~\cite{kaur2022} for $s$, false-abstention measurement --- and we
abstain when $p\le\alpha$; at $\alpha=0.05$ the measured rate is $0.050$
and the negative control fires at the nominal rate
(Table~\ref{tab:detect}).

The rule residual detects the genuinely silent failures and is strongest
on multipath, where it beats the head's epistemic score in every cell, in
either tail (Table~\ref{tab:detect}). Carrier offset turns out not to be silent: the
epistemic itself fires there, an energy shift the head represents. The
residual is deliberately quiet at low SNR, where the energy flag and the
epistemic both fire.

Selection and confirmation are separated on three axes (training,
generation, and shift-level); all six pre-registered predictions hold,
five in 20 of 20 cells.

A second and cheaper flag targets the inversion itself.
Sec.~\ref{sec:mech} locates the failure in the feature norm, and the norm
is read through the same conformal $p$-value machinery as the residual,
now in the upper tail of $\log\|\phi\|$: it exceeds its calibration level
exactly where the reported uncertainty falls (multipath, bandwidth
reduction) and stays at the nominal rate where the head still responds
correctly to a channel change (fading, the TDL families;
Table~\ref{tab:detect}). It costs one norm of a vector the model already
computes.

A third, the energy flag, covers the energy-loss family the first two
miss. It is an error regression: the same three curve statistics as in
\eqref{eq:rule}, refit to predict the log error instead of the reported
uncertainty; at inference its score reads the curve alone. It leads on both 3GPP NLOS
families while staying quiet on bandwidth loss, which the second flag
owns (Table~\ref{tab:detect}). All three are confirmed on generation
seeds never used to build them.

\subsection{Repair within the region gate, abstention beyond it}
The three flags are also the
three correctors, used as
normalizers~\cite{papadopoulos2002,papadopoulos2008} of the score
\eqref{eq:maha},
\begin{equation}
M_\kappa = M/\exp(\kappa z),
\label{eq:norm}
\end{equation}
with $z$ the assigned signal standardized on one half of the calibration
split (negative $z$ set to zero except for the energy flag) and the
quantile taken on the other, leaving the in-distribution
guarantee untouched (coverage $0.892$--$0.918$, every arm); $\kappa$ is a
design-time constant like $\alpha$, frozen per signal and confirmed on new
shift sets. The corrected region is
$\{\,y : M(X,y)\le Q_\kappa\,e^{\kappa z(X)}\}$, where $Q_\kappa$ is the
calibration quantile of $M_\kappa$; its area is evaluated against the gate
without deployment labels.

Repair follows detection in three senses. A signal repairs only where it
fires: six silent signal-family pairs move coverage by at most $0.09$,
regions unchanged. Firing alone is not sufficient: three pairs fire at
$0.86$--$1.00$ yet their median corrected regions exceed the gate.
Across conditions satisfying the median-area gate, coverage gain tracks
detection power (Spearman $0.80$--$0.98$ per signal, negative control
excluded).

Table~\ref{tab:detect} gives the confirmed assignment; the outcome per family tracks the two limits:
bandwidth loss ($\beta^{*}=0.02$) returns to nominal at $2\%$ of the
search area; peak mis-selection ($\beta^{*}=0.38$) reaches $0.71$ at its assigned
$\kappa$; none of the tested settings attains nominal coverage under the
median-area gate. The energy family --- fading, moderate carrier
offset, channel family --- is served by the energy flag, which holds
every energy axis inside the region gate at a single $\kappa$ and doubles
as the in-distribution corrector without SNR labels: it lifts the
lowest-SNR bin from $0.67$ to $0.91$ with the median region at $0.1\%$ of
the search area, a conditional deviation nearly matching the label-using
Mondrian ($0.013$--$0.024$ vs.\
$0.018$--$0.029$ per model, held-out sets). Deep
SNR shift starts with regions already at $16\%$ of the search area, and both
tested nonzero energy settings exceed the median-area gate; abstention
remains --- the axis the head's epistemic detects reliably.

The map from family to normalizer is fixed at design time; given it,
correcting a snapshot and measuring its region need no labels, and we
abstain when that region exceeds the gate. Median regions are $\le\!19\%$ of
the search area where we repair against $\ge\!235\%$ where we abstain, so any
gate between them classifies the nine conditions the same way. The energy flag serves as both detector and corrector; retaining signed
$z$ lets its normalization factor fall below or rise above one.

Under the same gate and frozen $\kappa$, classical integrity checks
provide an external control: a lag-domain parity residual (RAIM-style), a
peak-sharpness ratio, and correlation-shape tests (SQM-style),
preregistered on held-out shift sets. The parity residual is the strongest detector on the energy
family ($0.80$--$0.92$) --- the shifts the head itself represents. On
strong multipath, the head's largest inversion, no classical check
exceeds $0.52$ (parity and shape flat across severity) and none repairs
past $0.62$ against $0.71$: the
failure classical monitoring sees worst is the one best detected by the
head-aware diagnostic among the methods tested.

At that gate, accepted coverage is $0.53$--$0.58$ on multipath,
fading and TDL-A/C; carrier offset passes $21\%$ of snapshots at coverage
$0.44$, and no deep-SNR snapshot passes. Choosing the normalizer without
the family label is not evaluated, and both abstention and repair select
subsets of the stream, so selective calibration is left to future work.

% =====================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:concl}

We studied uncertainty failures in non-cooperative emitter geolocation
under distribution shift with frozen conformal calibration. Departures from
an in-distribution rule linking curve statistics to reported uncertainty
make these failures measurable without labels. Three signals built from
them detect the failing families and improve coverage, though not always to
nominal; the corrected region's size supports abstention without
certifying coverage or establishing an end-to-end deployment policy.
Evidence is synthetic; selective calibration and field data remain
open.
